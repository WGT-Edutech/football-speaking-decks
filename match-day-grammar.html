Topic 1 of 4

Adjectives

An adjective describes a noun — a player, a match, a stadium. It normally sits before the noun, or after verbs like be, look, feel, get.

Position

  • a fast striker  •  a brilliant goal  •  the wet pitch
  • The striker is fast.  •  The match looks brilliant.

-ed and -ing

  • -ed = how somebody feels: I was exhausted after the match.
  • -ing = the thing that causes the feeling: The match was exhausting.

Comparing

  • Comparative: -er or more → He is faster than his teammate.
  • Superlative: the -est or the most → She is the best player in the league.
  • Irregular: good → better → the best  |  bad → worse → the worst
  • Spelling: big → bigger  |  easy → easier  |  exciting → more exciting

Topic 2 of 4

Articles

English has three choices: a, an, and the — plus no article at all. Most mistakes come from leaving an article out, not from adding a wrong one.

a / an

  • Singular countable noun, first mention, one of many: a player, an goal
  • Use an before a vowel SOUND: an hour, an honour, a university

the

  • Both people know which one: The referee showed him a yellow card.
  • Second mention: We saw a goal. The goal was brilliant.
  • Only one exists, or a superlative: the sun, the best player in the league

no article (zero article)

  • Plural or uncountable, talking in general: — football boots, — football
  • Names of sports, games, languages: play — football, speak — English
  • Most names of people, cities, countries, clubs: — London, — Real Madrid

Topic 3 of 4

Will / Going to

Both talk about the future, but they do different jobs. The exam loves to test the difference.

will

  • A decision made at the moment: The phone is ringing — I will answer it.
  • A prediction with no evidence: I think our team will win.
  • A promise or an offer: I won't tell anyone. I will carry that for you.

going to

  • A plan or intention made before now: We are going to watch the final at my house.
  • A prediction with evidence: Look at those clouds — it is going to rain.

Quick test

  • Did you decide just now? → will
  • Did you plan it earlier? → going to
  • Can you see it happening? → going to

Topic 4 of 4

Present Simple vs Continuous

The difference is when, not how. Simple = always, facts, routines. Continuous = now, temporary, changing.

Present simple

  • Habits and routines: He plays football every weekend.
  • Facts and permanent situations: She knows every player.
  • Timetables: The match starts at eight.
  • Time words: always, usually, every day, on Sundays

Present continuous

  • Happening now: Listen! The crowd is singing.
  • Temporary situations: They are training for the final this week.
  • Changing situations: The team is getting better.
  • Time words: now, at the moment, right now, these days

Stative verbs

  • Verbs like know, like, want, need, love, have (possession) describe a state, not an action.
  • They stay in the simple form: I don't like losing. (NOT I am not liking losing)
